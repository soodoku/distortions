AMR and AHRQ were selected on September 12, 2026. Both messages below are drafts;
neither has been sent. Source eligibility and subsequent access decisions remain
in [source_register.csv](source_register.csv).

## AMR: discussion-group linkage

To: Alice Siu <asiu@stanford.edu>

Subject: AMR deliberative poll: linking respondents to discussion groups

Hi Alice,

I'm extending our BJPS paper on homogenization, polarization, and domination to
additional deliberation studies. The six-country AMR project would be a useful
addition.

We have the harmonized survey files and codebooks from the Stanford repository
(https://purl.stanford.edu/sb639ms2957). The remaining piece is a crosswalk from
the released respondent IDs to their actual small discussion groups. The released
`Group` variable identifies treatment versus control.

Could you share the respondent-to-room mapping, including session/topic identifiers
if membership changed, and any session-level attendance or reassignment flags?
A brief description of how rooms were assigned and which topics each session
covered would also help us link the responses to the right discussions. The
supplement's country attendance totals match the 1,280 treated respondents in
the release; we need to establish who discussed together.

Pseudonymous IDs and the existing file format are fine. If someone else holds
the mapping, could you point me to them?

Thanks,
Gaurav

Contact basis: [Stanford profile](https://fsi.stanford.edu/people/alice-siu?by_news_type=q_a).
The archive README also lists deliberation@stanford.edu as the dataset contact.
The downloaded [extended data](https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:sb639ms2957/Extended%20Data-%20to%20add%20to%20data%20repository_Deliberative%20Polling%20on%20AMR.pdf)
contains the questionnaire and aggregate attendance in Tables 2–3, but no
respondent-to-room crosswalk. Its MD5 matches the repository manifest:
`1f6e80f182c51a2349364c8a71ee7d2d`.

## AHRQ: participant data and assignment documentation

To: Coretta Mallery Lankford <clankford@air.org>

Subject: Data access: AHRQ Community Forum deliberative-methods trial

Dear Coretta,

I'm extending our BJPS paper, "Deliberative Distortions? Homogenization,
Polarization, and Domination in Small Group Discussions," to other deliberation
studies. Your AHRQ Community Forum trial would be particularly useful because
it compares several discussion methods within one project.

Is there an archive or access process for the deidentified participant data?
We would need:

- Linked pre/post item responses for the deliberation and reading-only arms,
  with demographics and the questionnaires/codebook.
- Actual discussion-group and session IDs, method, site, attendance, and the
  case studies covered by each group; any membership changes across sessions.
- Assignment documentation or code, including willingness/availability strata
  and indicators identifying the later nonrandom control assignments described
  in the paper. Records for assigned nonattenders would also help assess attrition.

Existing files in their original format are fine. We are studying changes in
within-group dispersion, extremity, and movement toward initial subgroup
positions. Could you direct me to the data custodian if that is someone else?

Many thanks,
Gaurav

Contact basis: AIR's [study page](https://air.org/resource/journal-article/effectiveness-public-deliberation-methods-gathering-input-issues)
identifies Coretta as its contact; her [AIR profile](https://www.air.org/experts/person/coretta-mallery-lankford)
links the trial. The professional email is published in a
[federal project document](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/DownloadDocument?objectID=134078801).
This avoids relying on the corresponding author's 2015 AIR address, since
[Kristin Carman is now listed at PCORI](https://www.pcori.org/people/kristin-carman-ma-phd).
The public article and author bibliography were checked; no participant archive
was verified. AHRQ's report download was inaccessible during this access pass.
